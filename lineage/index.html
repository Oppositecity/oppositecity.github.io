LINEAGE

A page written by a one-dimensional cellular automaton. Each line is born from the line above it.

the rule, read left to right: the three cells above, and the cell they make

The row is 31 cells wide, with an empty margin beyond each edge. A cell lives or dies by Wolfram's rule, read from the three cells above it: its left neighbour, itself and its right. Meaning decides only which word it is: the word closest to the living words above it. No word comes back within six lines, so the page keeps moving. The strip in the margin is the row itself, one bar to a cell, as tall as its line, so the rule's shape builds down the side as the words run beside it. A line in red marks where the rule changed.
