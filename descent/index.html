The Descent

One object, traced back through the systems and physical events it depends on. The upper tiers use H.T. Odum's emergy accounting: the total solar energy used, directly and indirectly, to make something, in solar emjoules (sej). Emergy measures cost, not value.

Sunlight is where the method stops. The page continues past it in ordinary physical units.

What changes and what doesn't

Trace a different object and the first three tiers would change. The sunlight tier and everything below the break would stay the same, because every object on Earth depends on the same sun, the same stellar history, and the same initial conditions.

The tiers are not a sum. Each one shows the scale of the system the tier above draws on, and only a small fraction of it went into the object. A cup of coffee uses about a hundred-thousandth of one farm-hectare's year, and a negligible share of the sun's output.

Emergy counts what was used to produce something. It says nothing about whether the thing is useful or worth having. Odum sometimes treated emergy as a measure of value. That move is the most disputed part of his method, and this page does not make it.

Georges Bataille argued that a society is defined less by how it produces than by how it spends the surplus energy it cannot use productively, and he traced that surplus to the sun. The lower tiers show that the sun's output is itself drawn from older stores.

Notes on the figures

Above the break: emergy estimates (sej) following Odum's method. They are order-of-magnitude, depend on where system boundaries are drawn, and vary between published baselines. Rain transformity and the geobiosphere baseline follow Odum (1996) and Brown et al. (2016).

Below the break: physical energies (J), order-of-magnitude. The Penrose figure is an estimate of initial-condition improbability, and its interpretation is contested.

Bars are log₁₀ on two separate scales, sej above the break and J below it. A bar is comparable only to bars in the same section.

H.T. Odum, Environment, Power, and Society (1971) and Environmental Accounting (1996). Georges Bataille, La Part maudite (1949). Roger Penrose, The Emperor's New Mind (1989).
